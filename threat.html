Threat model

ATC depends on correctly understood instructions from authorized sources. Legacy analog VHF voice carries no cryptographic source authentication, so the credibility of a transmission rests on phraseology, context, voice and the procedural checks around it.

Documented incidents

  • Tenerife, 1977. The KLM aircraft began takeoff without clearance; overlapping radio transmissions and inadequate language were among the contributing factors in the 583-fatality collision.
  • Roanoke, 1993. An individual impersonating an air traffic controller issued false flight instructions that one pilot began to follow, before legitimate controllers corrected the misinformation.
  • Melbourne, 2016. A Boeing 737 crew received an unauthorized go-around instruction at about 200 feet and initiated the maneuver, judging that questioning it could add confusion. The controller denied issuing it as the maneuver began; the aircraft landed safely. The incident does not establish the use of voice deepfakes.

Adversary assumptions

The scenario assumes access to a controller recording, a public TTS system, traffic context, and a means of transmitting an unauthorized VHF message. The false instruction in Figure 1 follows FAA altitude phraseology and takes the form of an amended clearance, which is part of normal operations.

A stronger adversary assumption is repeated access to intelligible recordings of an on-duty controller’s exchanges with aircraft over multiple shifts. Such access could provide a wider choice of complete utterances and more context for voice, cadence, and prosody. The brief excerpts used in this study do not model that collection scenario. Improved spoof credibility under those conditions remains untested here.

Operational defenses

The FAA requires acknowledgment of instructions and checking of items read back; its false-communication procedure calls for correcting information and alerting affected aircraft. FAA guidance also recommends altitude readback for mutual verification. In Melbourne, the investigation found minimal overall operational impact from the unlawful broadcasts.

Evaluation scope. The study evaluates speech generation on unchanged corpus transcripts. Pilot acceptance, aircraft response, over-the-air transmission and correction timing are outside the evaluation. The measured voice and delivery features characterize synthesis, not successful spoofing.
