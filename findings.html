Results use equal controller weights and conditional 95% intervals. Bold entries mark the better point estimate. Dark intervals exclude zero. Hover or focus a chart mark for its values.

Brief references, limited context

This study tests brief, word-boundary crops that may end before a sentence is complete. They provide limited context for a controller’s voice, prosody, and cadence. With Chatterbox’s inference controls held fixed, pacing and prosody remained inconsistent. Fine-tuning gave no consistent delivery advantage. Tuning the inference controls would be a separate intervention.

The ICNS 2026 paper notes that longer references typically improve speaker embeddings and identity conditioning. It also identifies pitch and cadence matching as persistent limitations.

A stronger adversary with intelligible recordings from multiple controller shifts could have fuller utterances to choose from. Optimizing spoof quality with that richer reference material, or testing its credibility to listeners, is outside this study’s scope.

Duration and pauses

Fine-tuning reduces mean output duration from 4.408 to 4.030 s (recorded speech averages 4.269 s). Mean excess over the recorded duration falls from 0.537 to 0.328 s, while mean shortfall rises from 0.398 to 0.566 s. Shortening can improve overly long outputs and worsen short ones. Pauses, pitch and predicted quality

  • Pause-fraction error changes by Δ=−0.0055 across all 270 targets [−0.0098, −0.0014]. A descriptive split shows reduced error for the 174 targets without detected pauses and increased error for the other 96.
  • Predicted naturalness (UTMOSv2) changes by −0.062 [−0.094, −0.029], indicating lower predicted naturalness. SCOREQ changes by −0.020 with an interval including zero.
  • Pitch-slope error increases by 0.371 semitones per utterance [0.052, 0.689] on the 162-message subset with valid pYIN values in every compared condition. Pitch support is uneven: only 2 of 20 and 7 of 50 messages for two equally weighted controllers.
  • Uncertain changes: envelope distance, pitch-median and pitch-range errors, 6-s speaker cosine and SCOREQ all have secondary intervals that include zero. The insufficient 2-s pitch-range support prevents that delivery comparison.
At 2 s, higher embedding similarity accompanied higher word and duration errors. Greater speaker-embedding similarity can therefore coexist with poorer word recovery and delivery.

Security implications

The earlier ICNS 2026 study by the same authors reported 3.9% mean WER and 0.726 mean speaker cosine across 1,000 ATCOSIM utterances with pretrained Chatterbox. Its Whisper large-v2 recognizer was fine-tuned on ATCO2 and ATCOSIM, whereas the large-v3 variant used here was fine-tuned on ATCO2, so the WER values are not directly comparable. It also found weak pitch-contour matching and measurable cadence deviations.

The two studies show that pretrained synthesis can condition speech on public controller recordings. The tested adaptation offers no confirmed overall advantage, even though some short-reference cosine scores improve. Familiar phraseology and voice resemblance could lend credibility to a spoofed instruction, but the operational outcome depends on the context and defenses in the threat model.

Limitations and future work

  • Intervals condition on the tested controllers, references, trained models and generation draws; shared training data and sessions limit population generalization.
  • Model and recognizer pretraining exposure is incompletely known. Isolating register effects requires matched non-ATC training controls.
  • WER weights all errors equally, although phraseology-consistent omissions differ operationally from incorrect numerical repetitions. Command understanding needs evaluation beyond WER.
  • UTMOSv2 and SCOREQ are prediction models, not listener ratings; no listening or simulator study was run.
  • Future work: combine speech analysis and instruction extraction with trusted clearance records, surveillance data and geographic constraints; run listening and simulator studies comparing genuine, base and adapted speech for unauthorized-instruction detection, false alarms, comprehension and correction timing, including unmodified public models.
