Abstract

Analog very high frequency (VHF) voice links in air traffic control (ATC) lack cryptographic source authentication. Public text-to-speech models can generate controller-conditioned speech for potential unauthorized instructions. We compare pretrained Chatterbox with versions fine-tuned on recordings from other controllers. Across 270 messages from 9 controllers at the 6-s reference limit, adaptation changes log-duration error by −0.0017 and word error rate (WER) by +0.684 percentage points, without confirming improved duration matching within a 1-percentage-point WER tolerance. On 90 matched messages, base synthesis yields 14.28% WER with 2-s references. Fine-tuning increases speaker-embedding similarity at 2- and 4-s limits, without confirmed improvement at 6 s. Predicted naturalness declines, and pitch-slope error increases on its measured subset. Public pretrained models already provide controller-voice conditioning, lowering the technical barrier to hypothetical voice spoofing. The tested adaptation establishes no consistent advantage.

Index terms: air traffic control, aviation security, text-to-speech synthesis, speaker similarity, domain adaptation.

01 · Overview

Brief recap

Prior study · ICNS 2026

Our prior ICNS paper provides the baseline for this ICASSP study. It evaluated pretrained Chatterbox on 1,000 ATCOSIM controller utterances, measuring word recovery, speaker resemblance, and delivery. Mean word error rate was 3.9%, and mean speaker-embedding cosine similarity was 0.726. Pitch contours, speaking rate, and pauses were less consistently reproduced than the words. Vu and Wei, ICNS 2026.

Building on that baseline, this ICASSP study tests whether fine-tuning improves controller speech synthesis when only brief reference audio is available. It uses matched base-model outputs as the experimental baseline, so scores from the two papers should not be treated as a direct before-and-after comparison.

TL;DR

Base and adapted models receive the same message, reference audio, and generation seed. The comparison measures how fine-tuning changes word recovery, voice resemblance, and delivery.

FINDING 1

Pretrained synthesis with brief references

14.28%WER, 2-s references

On the matched 90-message panel, unchanged pretrained Chatterbox reaches 14.28%, 14.54% and 14.80% word error rate with 2-, 4- and 6-s reference limits. Mean speaker cosine rises with reference length: 0.499, 0.556, 0.581.

FINDING 2

Fine-tuning did not meet the joint criterion

+0.684 ppΔWER at 6 s

Across 270 messages, adaptation changes log-duration error by −0.0017 [−0.0149, 0.0123] and WER by +0.684 percentage points [−0.001, 1.410]. Both intervals include zero, and the WER upper bound exceeds the 1-pp tolerance. The intervals also do not establish equivalence.

FINDING 3

Short-reference gains came with tradeoffs

+0.0139cosine at 2 s

Adaptation raises mean speaker cosine by 0.0139 at 2 s and 0.0098 at 4 s, with intervals excluding zero. Adapted scores still stay below base synthesis with 6-s references. At 2 s, WER rises 1.488 pp and log-duration error rises 0.022.

FINDING 4

Uneven changes in delivery

−0.062UTMOSv2

Mean output duration falls from 4.408 to 4.030 s. Excess duration decreases, while shortfall increases. Pause-fraction error fell mainly for targets without detected pauses. Pitch-slope error rose by 0.371 semitones per utterance on the 162-message subset with pitch support.
