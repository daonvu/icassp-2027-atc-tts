03 · Study design

Study design

Each native low-rank adaptation (LoRA) model is fine-tuned on controllers other than the evaluation speaker. Base and adapted models then generate the same messages with identical conditioning audio and generation seeds, and an ATC speech recognizer measures word recovery.

Data and controller separation

ATCOSIM: non-prompted speech recorded at controller headset microphones during real-time simulations. 9 of its 10 controllers contribute 90 development, 180 validation and 270 evaluation utterances.

  • Screening yields 5,881 training recordings (6.39 h).
  • Each native fit excludes every recording of the evaluation controller.
  • Two references per controller, cropped at reviewed word boundaries within 2-, 4- and 6-s limits; a separate 8-s comparison covers 5 controllers.
  • Received-radio conditioning: 10 controller-labeled ATCO2 recordings × 5 ATCOSIM texts.

Model adaptation

LoRA on the 120 attention projections of Chatterbox’s T3 speech-token predictor. The pretrained weights, speaker encoder and S3Gen waveform decoder stay fixed.

  • Rank 16, α = 32, dropout 0.05: 3,932,160 trainable parameters.
  • Token-normalized cross-entropy over speech and end-of-sequence tokens.
  • AdamW, batches of 20; learning rate warms linearly to 5×10⁻⁵ at update 33, then constant.
  • Selected dose: 33 updates (660 distinct utterances per fit), fixed before confirmation across 3 training seeds.

Paired synthesis

For each message and reference limit, the 2 references yield 2 base outputs and 6 adapted outputs (3 training seeds). A message-specific seed is reset before each output and shared across conditions.

  • Chatterbox public defaults held fixed: temperature 0.8, classifier-free guidance 0.5, exaggeration 0.5, repetition penalty 1.2, min-p 0.05, top-p 1.0, 1,000-token cap, 10 flow-matching steps.
  • Outputs are 24 kHz and retain the model’s watermarking.

Evaluation and inference

Outputs are averaged within messages, then within controllers, with equal controller weights. Conditional 95% intervals use 2,000 paired within-controller message bootstrap resamples.

  • Primary criterion: upper bounds UD < 0 for the duration-error change and UW < 0.01 for the WER change.
  • Secondary: pause fraction, envelope distance (2–12 Hz), pYIN pitch median/range/slope, ECAPA-TDNN cosine, UTMOSv2, SCOREQ.
  • Secondary intervals are unadjusted for multiple comparisons.

Why 33 updates? Development checkpoint selection

Before confirmation, checkpoints at 16, 33, 66, 132, 264 and 528 updates were evaluated on the 90 held-out development messages under the first training seed. A checkpoint was eligible if it had complete measurements, reduced mean duration error (upper bound below 0) and a WER upper bound below the 1-pp tolerance. Among eligible checkpoints, the one with the lowest duration error was chosen. Longer fits improve development duration error further but fail the WER condition.
